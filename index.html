Vortex · Continuum Build
03 / 09

Vortex · Continuum Build

Working alpha prototype · In active development

VORTEX explores recursive kinetic synthesis: an audiovisual environment in which electronic sound and evolving geometry are shaped together. Eight synthesis engines, per-engine sequencing and expressive kinetic controls bring bass, acid, FM textures and layered motion into one playable prototype. Live audio analysis animates the visual field, while geometric relationships can become modulation sources for the sound. Open the instrument with Filthy Skunk Feral_SEQ already loaded, ready for Play.

Explore this instrument
  • Eight synthesis engines with analogue, wavetable, FM, phase, granular, noise, acid and hybrid sound-generation modes
  • Up to 256 sequence steps per engine, with note, gate, accent, slide and modulation editing
  • Audio-responsive toroidal, spiral, Apollonian, Koch, spherical, Flower of Life and particle visualisations
  • Geometry-derived modulation from curvature, scale, proximity, structural detail and angular motion
  • Optional advanced recursive coupling for filter movement, pitch, FM interaction, resonance and saturation
  • Bounded, smoothed feedback between note-event energy and geometric modulation
  • Keyboard and MIDI performance, modulation routing, pattern arrangement and JSON preset export/import
  • Filthy Skunk Feral_SEQ loaded on opening; press Play to start the supplied sequence

How the recursion works: live spectrum and level analysis make the visuals respond to the sound. Separately, the geometry is described mathematically, producing control signals that can alter synthesis. In advanced mode, a smoothed signal derived from note-event energy also influences those geometric calculations, allowing the next modulation stage to respond to earlier musical activity. Recursive depth changes the structure of that influence; it is not a direct conversion of rendered pixels into audio. The deeper feedback and sonic-influence controls are optional and bounded. This is a working alpha build still being developed; features, workflow and performance remain under refinement.

Beyond the instruments · The Continuum architecture

One instrument can stand alone. Several can form a performance rig. Ultimately, the individual components can become nodes within one connected Continuum.

Continuum is designed not simply to generate sound, but to become part of the signal chain of a complete electronic or live performance environment, bringing synthesis, rhythm, sequencing, mixing, signal routing and MIDI control into an interconnected system.

MIDI and audio connectivity allow external instruments and controllers to enter that environment alongside voices, guitars, acoustic and electronic instruments, effects, amplification and PA systems. For a singer or guitarist, Continuum can become part of a compact live-performance rig: combining external sound with electronic instruments and backing material, balancing sources and routing the resulting performance onwards to a PA. For an electronic musician, different Continuum instruments can be distributed across multiple devices, with synthesis on one, rhythm on another and sequencing or specialist engines elsewhere.

For live performance, this creates a portable environment in which musical elements can be introduced, removed, balanced and manipulated as the performance develops rather than simply playing back a fixed arrangement.

The wider architecture extends that principle through daisy-chaining musical architecture. A synthesis engine can connect to a sequencer; a rhythm system can join it; another Continuum instrument can contribute a different synthesis architecture; and MIDI hardware or external instruments can enter the chain. Signals can then be routed, mixed, processed and passed onwards through the performance system.

This modular approach avoids requiring one enormous application to contain every possible instrument. Standalone components provide focused environments in which particular synthesis methods, interfaces, workflows and performance protocols can be developed independently, while remaining capable of becoming interoperable parts of the wider Continuum architecture.

The aim is for every appropriate element of Continuum to be able to connect with the others without requiring the musician to become a systems engineer simply to make music.

Alongside the browser-based instruments, a future development direction is to create installable Continuum software synthesizers for use inside musicians’ preferred DAWs, allowing selected Continuum instruments and synthesis architectures to become part of established studio and production workflows as dedicated soft synths.

At its centre, Continuum Mosaic already explores the integration of synthesis, rhythm, sequencing, routing and performance control. Its patching, mixing, effects and final-bus architecture demonstrates how the individual elements can operate as parts of a larger system, while the wider Continuum project extends that principle towards a distributed modular environment.

A modular musical environment whose scale and structure are determined by the person playing it.

CONTINUUM VISION

CONTINUUM is an expanding ecosystem of fully playable audiovisual instruments, synthesis architectures, rhythmic technologies and experimental sound environments, each developed with its own creative identity and capabilities.

Our wider vision is to bring these existing technologies together through progressive refinement, consolidation and integration, creating a unified, expandable modular workstation for sound design, composition, live performance and audiovisual exploration.

Rather than replacing the individual instruments, this evolution will allow their synthesis engines, sequencers, rhythm systems, effects, modulation and performance controls to become interconnected components within a larger creative environment.

Envisioned as a flexible, potentially multi-screen and multi-device workspace, CONTINUUM will support shared timing, audio and MIDI routing, cross-instrument modulation, signal processing and coordinated performance, while preserving the independence and character of each instrument.

The ambition is an adaptable creative architecture in which individual technologies can operate independently or combine into increasingly sophisticated configurations, bringing the expressive possibilities of an extensive modular studio into one cohesive, evolving environment.
